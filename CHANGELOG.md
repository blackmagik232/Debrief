# Changelog

## 2.16.0

- **Racing** (new page, after Practice in the sidebar): your starts and your incidents across all your races, from the replay scans, telemetry files and saved reviews already on your PC. Debrief only reads them (RaceCap's folder too, when you have linked it); nothing is written there and nothing goes online.
- **Your starts**: for every race, where you started and where you were after lap 1, the places gained or lost (+ or −), your incidents on lap 1 and whether you were in contact (Yes, Probably, Possibly or No), with a link to the saved review when there is one. Each race says where its positions came from: the official results saved with the review, RaceCap's results file, iRacing's own position at the green from your telemetry or the replay, or (last, and labelled as a guess) the grid worked out from the replay. In a multi-class race the positions are in class.
- **Corner by corner on lap 1**: for a race with a replay scan (Scan this race on a review), the places seen changing between each corner, and a bar per corner of places gained and lost over your scanned races. Only cars the replay saw near you can be counted, so these can add up to less than your real change, and the page says when they do. A race without a scan still shows its start and lap 1 positions from telemetry.
- **Your incidents**: every incident in your telemetry (iRacing counts 1x off track, 2x loss of control, 4x contact; two at once are split), placed on the track by corner, with the lap, the session (race, qualifying or practice) and what kind it probably was: **Your own** (no other car near), **Contact** (iRacing counted contact or a car was within 10 m) or **Caught up** (another car's trouble started first in the same spot). With a replay scan it names who was close, as "probably" or "possibly": the replay shows where the cars were, not who touched whom. A grid of corners against kinds shows the counts and points, lap 1 against later, and the full list says why each one was called what it was. Points added while no telemetry was recording are counted but can't be placed, and the page says so. Pace lap incidents are kept and marked.
- **Patterns only when they hold up**: "you usually lose places at Turn 1" or "Turn 6 is where your incidents keep happening" is only said with at least 3 races showing it and a fair test (every sign flip or shuffle, counted over every corner tested) saying chance alone would show it less than 1 time in 20. With fewer races the page only says what happened. A trend over time needs 5 races.
- It is worked out when you open the page (about a second for a few races) and kept, so it opens at once next time; a new scan, review or telemetry file is picked up by itself. **Look again** checks for new files.
- On your real files (McLaren 570S GT4 at Zolder): 3 races. P5 to P3 (+2), P17 to P16 (+1) and P28 to P16 (+12), all positions from your telemetry; the scanned race shows places changing at the start, Turn 2, Turn 4, Turn 5 and Turn 7 (+7 seen of the +12), with contact on lap 1. 25 incidents over 9 sessions: 21 at Zolder (48 points; Turn 1 has the most points, 11), 11 your own, 8 contact, 2 caught up, 13 points not placed; 4 off tracks in one practice session at Oulton Park. No pattern yet: too few races.
- Not proven yet: the incident kinds and lap 1 corner counts have only been checked on one scanned race of yours and on made-up races; contact partners have never been checked against what really happened in a replay; and a telemetry folder with hundreds of files hasn't been timed (the first opening reads every file once).

## 2.15.0

- **How the car handles** (Setup Lab, under Try next): a map of every corner on the way in, in the middle and on the way out, marked **Pushes** (understeer: the front slides first), **Rotates** (oversteer: the rear slides first), **Neutral** or **Not clear yet**, in words as well as colour, with one sentence that sums it up and how often you had to catch the rear (countersteer). Debrief learns how much steering this car normally needs in gentle bends from your own laps, then looks at how much you used near the limit. The car's rotation follows the wheel a few hundredths of a second later (0.07 to 0.08 s on your McLaren files), so Debrief measures that lag in each file and compares each moment's steering with the rotation it produced (without this, a test with no real effect called "pushes" in about half of all sessions). Pushes or Rotates is only shown when a shuffle test across your laps says it isn't luck (with the error in that learned steering counted in), it is big enough to feel (at least half a degree at the front wheels, about 10 degrees of steering wheel on the McLaren, and at least 20 % of the steering used there, so a car whose steering simply quickens in hairpins doesn't read as pushing), and the same reading from sideways g agrees. In tests with no real effect, false Pushes or Rotates anywhere on the map came up in at most 4 sessions in 100, with a 0.04 or 0.07 s lag, bends at several speeds and 10 % progressive steering. The way in is compared with your own typical corner entry, not with steady cornering: under braking every car turns in with less steering (about 17 degrees less on your typical entry at Zolder), so a corner is only marked when its way in differs from your usual entry, and a note under the map says so. A catch (countersteer) only counts when you held at least 12 degrees of opposite lock for a quarter of a second while the car kept rotating, not a quick change of direction where the wheel simply leads the car. It reads your newest setup's files (pick another setup, or all of them, from the list), and is worked out once and kept, so it opens at once the next time.
- **Setup ideas**: for the clearest findings, one conventional change at a time with the actual numbers from your setup ("Front anti-roll bar 2 → 1"), why it helps, what to try if it doesn't, and how sure it is (**Worth a try** or **A guess**), ending "Change only this, then drive at least 6 clean laps and compare again in Setup Lab". It only says "Try one change" for a change your setup can make: when the usual one can't be (a bar already at its lowest, or no such setting), it offers the other end of the car and says why, or says plainly there is nothing to change; ideas that can be made come first. Values like "P2" keep their letter, traction control reads "Traction control TC1 3 → 4", and a traction control idea says the number runs the other way on some cars. In a fixed setup series it says to adapt the driving instead.
- **Tyre pressures over time** (Setup Lab): your runs grouped by track temperature (5 °C bands) with the cold pressure, the hot pressure it came up to and your typical lap. "Your fastest runs were on X cold" is only said when you drove the pressures back and forth (each in at least 2 separate goes, A, B, A, B) and a shuffle test across those goes agrees; runs straight after each other on one pressure on one day count once. One pressure per day, or one go each after the other, is listed but not compared ("the day can't be separated from the pressure"): in tests where only the day mattered, the old run-by-run test called a winner in 6 sessions in 10, now in none. With 4 or more runs with hot pressures, **Hot pressure from cold**: type a cold front and rear pressure (and the track temperature) and Debrief says what they come up to, with its typical miss, checked on runs left out, and warns when the cold pressures you typed are outside what your runs show.
- **Share what changed** (Setup Lab > What changed): a short text of what you changed and what the laps said, with a Discord version in bold, each with a **Copy** button. No names or file names (a setup name with brackets of its own, like "Swazi Williams (v2) quali", is removed whole). Debrief never posts it anywhere: you paste it yourself.
- **Compare with a teammate's lap** (a Practice session page and the Coach page): pick a teammate's telemetry file (.ibt) for the same car and track. Debrief checks it for anything harmful first (a program hidden anywhere inside, a file that isn't really telemetry, the wrong car or track are refused in plain words), takes their best clean lap and deletes its temporary copy straight away. The session page then shows Corner coach cards **against their lap**. Tick **Keep for this car and track** to keep the worked-out lap (never the file) for next time, or untick it to remove the saved lap; picking another file without Keep never deletes a lap you kept (it says it is still kept). **Forget it** removes it.
- **Coach against their lap** (Coach page, off by default): the live coach uses your teammate's best lap as its reference when you start it in that car at that track, and your own new bests don't replace it there (in another car or at another track they still update your own). The Coach page says whose lap it is using.
- On your real files (McLaren 570S GT4 at Zolder, 14 clean laps on one setup): the car pushes in the middle of Turn 1 and, more than on your other entries, on the way into Turn 5; 11 places are neutral and the rest not clear yet. You caught the rear once, on the way out of Turn 7 (23 degrees of opposite lock for 0.35 s at full throttle). The three "catches" at Turn 5 an earlier build reported were the chicane's change of direction, with the car's rotation following the wheel. Setup ideas: front anti-roll bar 2 → 1 for Turn 1. Tyre pressures: 2 runs, both on 176 kPa, so nothing to compare yet and no prediction (it needs 4 runs). Share has nothing yet (one setup).
- Not proven yet: the balance map needs your laps on more setups before its setup ideas can be checked against what actually happened; the wheelbase used to turn the learned steering into a steering ratio is partly from memory (it only changes that one sentence, not the map); and teammate files have only been checked with your own file standing in for a teammate's, never a real teammate's file.

## 2.14.0

- **This session's one thing** (top of Practice): Debrief picks one change for your next session at that car and track from the Corner coach, with a number to aim for and the check that counts a lap as a hit ("We'll count laps where your peak brake in Turn 4 stays at or below 88%"), how to try it, how sure it is (Clear pattern, Likely, Few laps or Experiment) and how many laps it comes from. **Coach this** hands it to the live coach; **Pick another** picks a different corner. Plans are kept per car and track, in a `practice` folder next to your settings.
- **Honest plans**: a plan only aims at a number the Corner coach actually tested for that tip, and it is never surer than the tip itself (a tip's second idea is at most Likely; Few laps says so in the text). Where your best laps are quicker but the Corner coach found no clear reason, the plan says **Try this and see** and is marked as an experiment, not a known fix. A plan you keep from last time whose tip has gone from your newer laps becomes an experiment too; if the tip is still there but your typical laps have caught up with your best, it says "You've closed most of this gap in Turn 4: moving on." and picks the next thing. Not enough laps of one kind on one setup: it says to drive more laps.
- **Scored afterwards**: open the session you drove next and **Your one thing** says "You hit it on 7 of 10 laps", with a square per lap, whether the laps that hit it were quicker through that corner and **Next: keep this** (under 7 laps out of 10) or **move to Turn 1**. Quicker is only said when a shuffle test agrees (hit and missed laps shuffled 400 times, a gap as big in fewer than 1 shuffle in 20, at least 0.03 s, and at least 3 laps each way); it describes those laps and never promises lap time. Laps are only compared with laps from the same part of the session (runs of 5 or 6 laps in a row), so a session that simply gets quicker as the track and fuel change no longer looks like the plan working (in a test with no real effect, "Quicker" came up in about 3 sessions in 100 instead of up to 41). A carry-more-speed plan is timed from the corner to 150 m past it, since more speed in the corner can cost the exit. For a "drive it the same way every lap" experiment the score gives your median time through the corner against your typical time before. A session is only scored once it has at least one clean lap through the plan's corner; otherwise the plan waits for the next one. The Practice page shows the last session's report card and every plan so far with its hit rate.
- **The coach watches it**: with a plan for the car and track iRacing shows, the coach measures that one corner after each clean pass, shows "Hit 3 of 5 so far" on the Coach page and now and then says a short word ("Good brake point", "Too much brake"), within its usual limits on how often it talks. It stays quiet when a pass is too close to the line to call (within 5 m for a brake point, half the margin for a pedal %, a speed or a time), so it never says the opposite of what the session score says later (played back on your Zolder files: 0 of 515 words disagreed; before this, about 4 in 100, and 1 in 5 for corner times). That corner is matched to the coach's own corner by where it is on track, and the plan's word takes the place of that corner's other cues. A pass with a gap in the telemetry is left out, the coach reads iRacing at least 15 times a second while it watches a plan, and a new plan (Pick another, or a score) is picked up while the coach is running.
- **Mute from a wheel button**: Coach page > Settings: press **Learn**, then the button on your wheel within 15 seconds (Windows only passes on the first 32 buttons of each device). It mutes and unmutes the coach while it is on. **Clear** removes it. It reads the wheel through Windows' own game controller interface, only while the coach is on (or while learning). While the wheel answers it reads just that one button; unplugged, it looks for it again after 2 seconds, then less often, up to every 30 seconds.
- **Setup checklist** on the start page: Garage 61 token, telemetry folder, read your telemetry, 5 clean laps in one session, test the coach's voice, each with a tick or a button to do it. **Hide** it any time; **Help > Setup checklist** brings it back. Test voice ticks its step when the voice is heard.
- On your real files (McLaren 570S GT4 at Zolder) there is no Corner coach tip yet, so the first plan is an experiment: "Try this and see: drive Turn 3 the same way every lap" (your best laps are quicker there, but with no clear reason why yet).
- Not proven yet: wheel button mute hasn't been tried with a real wheel (only with a stand-in for Windows' controller interface), and the coach's spoken progress words for the one thing haven't been heard in the car (they were checked by playing telemetry files back as if live).

## 2.13.0

- **Practice** (new page, Ctrl+0): every time you drove, straight from the telemetry files iRacing saves on your PC, newest first, without waiting for Garage 61. Open one to see your best and typical lap, how consistent you were, and how it compares with last time at that track.
- **Best possible lap**: your best time through each part of the lap added up, with a bar for where the time is. It now counts only clean laps once there are 5 of them (before, warm-up and slow laps could make it 0.6 to 0.9 s too quick), and says so. It's a guide, not a lap you can count on.
- **Corner coach**: for each corner, what you do differently on your best laps (brake later, press less hard, come off the brake earlier...), how to try it, the speeds it changes, why it works and the laps it comes from, with how sure it is (Clear, Likely, Few laps). Taken from your recent laps of that car at that track. Corners with no clear pattern, corners already close to your best and your braking habits are listed too.
- **Honest statistics in the Corner coach**: a tip is only given when it passes a fair test. Debrief shuffles which laps count as your best 400 times; if shuffled laps show a pattern as clear in 1 shuffle in 20 or more, it says "no clear pattern yet" instead (before, on your real laps, plain scatter made a tip in about 1 corner in 3; now about 1 in 25). Clear means fewer than 1 in 100, from 12 or more laps, and still passing with every corner counted at once. Brake-point tips need 10 clean laps. Race laps and practice or qualifying laps are compared separately, and so are setups, and each tip says which laps it used.
- **What the corner time means**: "your best laps were about 0.12 s quicker here" is each corner's share of the gap between your quickest third of laps and the rest, so the corners never add up to more than that gap. It describes your own laps; it is not a promise of lap time (part of any best lap is luck), so the cards no longer say "s a lap".
- ABS words only for a car whose ABS actually worked in your laps; without it the braking habits say to press until just before the wheels lock.
- The session page also shows every lap (counted, or why not), fuel per lap and how far a tank goes (and, when the race needs more than a tank, the stop and how much to add), the track conditions and the setups you used, with a link to Setup Lab for that car. Consistency says how many valid laps were left out as warm-up or slow, and gives the spread over all valid laps beside the clean ones. Against last time, typical laps are only compared when both sessions have 3 or more clean laps, and "a new best" needs 0.05 s or more.
- **Coach these corners**: the live coach now focuses on the Corner coach's corners for that car and track, and after each one says its short tip, one idea in a few words ("T4: lighter brake"). "Brake later" is only said on a lap where you braked early there, never after braking too late. The Coach page shows which corners those are. Without Corner coach tips it works as before, from your race reviews.
- The coach keeps the ABS reading when its reference lap comes from a telemetry file.
- Corners read the same way on the session page: **Turn 3**, with the race review's name **(C3)** in small text, in the best possible lap bars, the Corner coach cards and the text. On the Coach page, its corners show as "Turn 3 (from the Corner coach)". The Corner coach's tips use colons and full stops instead of dashes.
- Not proven yet: the Corner coach tips and the spoken tips haven't been tried in the car. On your real files (14 clean laps at Zolder, 11 of them race laps) the Corner coach now gives no tip yet: with the shuffle test, no corner shows a pattern beyond your lap-to-lap scatter, so it lists them under "no clear pattern yet". More laps of one kind will tell. The best possible lap was still 0.76 s under your best at Zolder on Nov 1 (5 clean laps, each part's best from a different lap) and 0.65 s on Nov 2 (only 3 clean laps, so it counts all valid laps and says it may be too quick).

## 2.12.0

- **Setup Lab** (new page, Ctrl+8): Debrief reads the telemetry files iRacing saves on your PC (only reads them, never changes them) and shows your setups (A, B, C), what changed between them in plain words, which one was faster (only when the clean laps clearly say so; otherwise how many more laps would tell; and if the faster one was simply driven later, it says part of that may be you getting quicker), one change to try next with how sure it is, and notes on readings it left out. Fixed setup series just say so. You can tell it whether a series is open or fixed. The first read goes through every file, then only new ones.
- **Check a setup file** (Setup Lab > More): a setup from someone else is checked for programs and scripts before you use it. Debrief never opens, runs or installs it.
- **Coach** (new page, Ctrl+9): a few short spoken cues while you drive, from your own best lap and the corners your race reviews say cost you time. Quiet in races unless you switch that on, and never on lap 1. Mute with the button or Ctrl+Shift+M. Settings for what it says, volume, speed and an optional brake beep, and Test voice.
- Prepare shows small links to **Setup Lab for this car** (when there are telemetry files for it) and the **Coach**.
- Settings > Setup Lab and Coach: pick iRacing's folder if your telemetry isn't in Documents\iRacing\telemetry.
- Not proven yet: the coach hasn't been used in the car yet; the Windows voice hasn't been heard on your PC yet (Test voice is the first check); and "quiet when a car is alongside" only works live, because iRacing doesn't record the spotter in its telemetry files, so it can't be tried from a saved file.
