# Debrief

**Debrief · by Black Magik Studios**

© 2026 Black Magik Studios. Free to use; see LICENSE.

Reviews your iRacing races from Garage 61 and keeps a library of every review, in its own window.

**New in 2.30:** **Endurance Race Room**: a Race Room page (under Coach) for team and long races, for the driver and for a teammate on their own PC. A pit board with the crew chief's call, class position, laps and time left, box by, the cars around you and your last lap; who is driving and the stint timer; the next driver and when their stint starts in their own time zone, from your saved stint plan; on plan, ahead or behind; a swap card (fuel, tyres, repairs, incidents, next stint targets); the rest of the race worked out again after a caution, a long stop or a late driver, with options side by side; every class rival's stops; a Race Room line on the overlay. A teammate's fuel is always marked estimated and never spoken. If you are the next driver your PC says "Your stint starts in about fifteen minutes" and "about five minutes". After the race, **Race review** shows pace by stint and driver, stops against the plan, fuel, traffic and rivals, joining your teammates' logs. **2.29:** **Crew chief**: in a race, Debrief reads your car and your class live and tells you what a crew chief would. A pit board on the Coach page and a line on the overlay show your fuel laps, laps to go, when to box (a window) and how much to add, how you stand against your saved stint plan, the car ahead and the car behind. A short voice calls "Box in three laps", "Box this lap, forty-two litres", "Box now, cheap stop" under a caution, "Car behind closing, three tenths a lap" and "Car alongside", never under braking and never with a car alongside. Turn the calls or the voice off in **Settings** on the Coach page; fuel follows **Settings > Units**. **2.27:** **Live data**: Debrief records each iRacing session in the background (fuel, driver aids, pit stops, tyres, incidents and every car's pit stops), shows it beside your Garage 61 sessions as **From iRacing**, and adds a **From iRacing** section to race reviews. Switch it on or off, and start it with Windows (a recorder with a small icon near the clock), in **Settings > Live data**; recordings are kept in `Recorded` inside your reviews folder and never deleted. **2.26:** **Plan stints** for long races: on Prepare (or from Plan fuel), set the race length, tank, pit lane and refuelling times, and up to 8 drivers with their own lap time, fuel use, the race hours they can drive and their own clock. Debrief works out every stint, who drives it, the fuel to add, tyres, the 30-second driver swap and when the flag falls, checks iRacing's fair share, minimum and maximum drivers and your tyre limit, and compares saving fuel to drop a stop and double-stinting tyres over the same distance. **Save for this track** and **Copy plan** for your team chat. The Coach page now says how much of your PC the coach uses, and Copy diagnostics includes Debrief's memory. **2.23:** **Set-Up Lab gives setup advice on its own**: open it (or a Practice session) and Debrief has already read your clean laps for understeer, oversteer, a car that is unsettled on the way in and poor traction on the way out, says whether each looks like the **setup** or your **driving**, and offers one ranked change at a time with a reason, how sure it is, and an optional **How did the car feel?** answer. **2.19:** an optional see-through **coach overlay** on top of iRacing (Coach > Overlay; needs borderless windowed mode), Setup Lab waits for 3 separate stints on a setup before naming a faster one, and plain words for the remaining jargon. **2.18:** **Car vs car**: which car of a class is quicker at a track for drivers of similar iRating (only said when the numbers hold up), you vs drivers in the same car, and where you were faster in each car from your telemetry. Also new in 2.18: Debrief opens on **Today** (your #1 thing, your last race and last practice), the sidebar is grouped and **F1** or **?** lists the keys (**Ctrl+Enter** reviews your last race), speeds are **mph or km/h** as you choose in **Settings > Units**, the Coach has a **driving view** to read from the car, and a race review or Practice session can be **compared with a teammate's lap**. **2.17.2:** the **coach talks more**: pick **Quiet**, **Normal**, **Busy** or **Only when I ask**; it says a word after corners where you lost or gained time, sector times, learns your focus corners from the session, builds a reference from your first clean lap, and tells you your pace when you ask (**Ctrl+Shift+P** or a wheel button). Every cue is checked once more just before it plays. **2.17:** **Coach 2.0**: the live coach speaks with a natural voice (four to pick from), through the headset you choose, with a **coach log** you can copy for Claude, **Rehearse** with a session, **Repeat that** and **Mute for 1 minute**. **2.16:** **Racing**: one page for your **starts** (places gained or lost on lap 1, corner by corner from a replay scan) and your **incidents** (which corners, what kind, how sure), across all your races. **2.15:** **Car and setup**: Setup Lab shows **how the car handles** in every corner (where it pushes or rotates, with one setup change to try at a time), **tyre pressures over time** (and what a cold pressure comes up to), and **Share what changed** to paste in Discord; compare with, or be coached against, a **teammate's lap** from their telemetry file. **2.14:** the **Practice loop**: Practice picks **one thing** to work on next session at a track, with a number you can check; the coach watches that corner and keeps count; afterwards the session page scores it ("You hit it on 7 of 10 laps") and says whether to keep it or move on. Also: mute the coach from a **wheel button**, and a **setup checklist** on the start page. **2.13:** **Practice** reviews every session straight from your telemetry files, with the best possible lap and the **Corner coach** (one thing to try in each corner, which the live coach then says while you drive). **2.12:** **Setup Lab** reads the telemetry files iRacing saves on your PC and shows what changed between your setups, which one was faster and one change to try next; the **Coach** says a few short cues while you drive, from your own best lap. **2.11:** Prepare is one clean page: pick a car, series, track and week at the top and everything below follows. **2.10:** a season plan and sharper pre-race brief on Prepare, and Read a rival's lap from a replay for Compare. Earlier: **New in 2.7.5:** Scan this race on any race review: with its replay loaded in iRacing, Debrief fast-forwards through it (recording nothing) and adds a race log of every pass, fight, incident and pit stop. Also: load iRacing's results file instead of typing a result; a Resolve marker file for any review; Help (tour, what the words mean, keys). If you also use RaceCap, point Settings > RaceCap link at its library folder: reviews gain a race log (passes, fights, incidents, pit stops, position by lap), race stories, a traffic check and rival laps. Debrief only reads RaceCap's race folders, never its settings or keys, and everything works the same without it.

## Install

You need Windows 10 or 11. Microsoft Edge is used for the Debrief window (it comes with Windows).

1. Download **Debrief-Setup-x.y.z.exe** from the newest release at **github.com/blackmagik232/debrief/releases**. No GitHub account needed. Don't use the green Code button (that's the source code, not the app).
2. Double-click it. Windows will probably show a blue **"Windows protected your PC"** box, because the installer isn't code-signed. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. Click through the installer. It installs just for you (no admin password needed), adds Debrief to the Start menu and, if you leave the box ticked, to your desktop. Leave **Launch Debrief** ticked at the end.
4. Debrief opens on a short getting-started guide. Practice, Setup Lab, the Coach and Racing work straight away, from the telemetry iRacing saves on your PC; only race reviews, your Garage 61 history and team features wait for Garage 61:
   - **Turn on telemetry in iRacing.** iRacing doesn't save telemetry until you turn it on: in the car, press **Alt+L** (or turn on telemetry recording in iRacing's settings), then drive a few laps. The checklist shows whether Debrief found your telemetry folder and any telemetry files (.ibt) in it.
   - Make a free account at **garage61.net** and connect your iRacing account. If you're on a team, ask a teammate to invite you to it on Garage 61 (see [Teams on Garage 61](#teams-on-garage-61) below).
   - Request a personal access token at **garage61.net/developer**. It's free for personal, non-commercial use. Garage 61 checks each request by hand, so it can take a little while. Practice, Setup Lab, the Coach and Racing work while you wait.
   - Paste the token into Debrief and click **Save and check**. Debrief checks the connection straight away, then starts downloading your history in the background. If the check fails, the steps stay open and say why once. Waiting for Garage 61's email? Close Debrief; the steps are still there when you come back.
   - Click **Review last race** (or press **Ctrl+Enter**) once your race has uploaded to Garage 61.

**Updating:** Debrief tells you when a new version is out (**Update now** in the sidebar). You can also download the newer installer and run it. It installs over the old version and keeps your token, settings and reviews.

**Uninstalling:** Windows Settings > Apps > Debrief > Uninstall. Your reviews (`Documents\Debrief Reviews`) and your settings (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Debrief`) are left alone. Delete those folders yourself if you want them gone.

In the installed app, your token, settings, saved Garage 61 data and `debrief.log` live in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Debrief` (paste that into the File Explorer address bar to open it). Settings > About in Debrief shows the exact folder and the version, and **Copy diagnostics** copies a short report (version, counts and the end of the log, with your token, webhook, e-mail and Windows user name taken out) to paste when something goes wrong. In the installed app your Garage 61 token and Discord webhook are stored encrypted with your Windows account (DPAPI), so a copied settings file is useless on another PC or account; if Debrief ever says your saved token can't be opened, paste it again. Everything below about the Debrief folder applies to that folder instead.

## First-time setup (from source)

The source-only launchers live in the `dev` folder (see `dev/README.md`).

1. Run `dev\Setup.bat` (creates `.venv` next to the code and installs the requirements).
2. Run `dev\Install shortcut.bat` for a desktop icon, or start the window with `.venv\Scripts\python -m debrief.desktop`.
3. Debrief opens on the same getting-started guide as the installed app: paste your Garage 61 token and click **Save and check**.

## Using it

- **Today** (**Ctrl+1**), where Debrief opens: **Your #1 thing right now**, the one most useful thing to work on (from Practice's one thing, your newest review's Fix first, or a lap 1 habit on Racing), then your last race and your last practice, and links to the rest. Before you have any data it shows the setup steps instead. The sidebar groups the pages: Before you drive (Prepare, Coach), After you drive (All sessions, Practice, Racing), Car (Setup Lab, Car vs car), Over time (Progress, with Season, Skills and Goals under it) and Team.
- **Review last race** (**Ctrl+Enter**): finds your newest race on Garage 61, builds the review and opens it. Progress shows in the window, with a **Cancel** button; if you are offline it gives up in about 10 seconds and offers **Open saved reviews**. If your newest race is already reviewed it opens at once, and a banner says **Newer race found** when Garage 61 has one.
- **This week's race** (on Today): a card with **Prepare this race** for the series you follow, or **Pick your series**. Prepare's car list starts with that series' cars; **Show all cars** lists the rest.
- **Driving view**: **Exit driving view** (or **Esc**) brings the window back.
- **On a review**, **Add race result** and **Share card** stay on the bar; the rest (Resolve markers, Scan this race, Read a rival's lap, Compare with a teammate's lap, Post to Discord) are in **More**.
- **Settings** saves switches and lists by itself (a small tick says **Saved**); only text boxes have a Save button, and Debrief asks before you leave with unsaved text.
- **All sessions**: every race, qualifying and practice session you've driven, newest first, with laps and best lap. Click **Review** on any of them (practice and qualifying reviews get their own folder next to the race's).
  - **History sync**: the first time, Debrief downloads your whole Garage 61 history. It's paced at about one request a second, so a big history takes a while; you can keep using Debrief meanwhile, and if you close it the sync carries on from where it stopped next time. After that, Debrief catches up each time it opens (only new laps, usually a few requests) and **Sync now** does it on demand. Starting a review pauses a running sync.
  - It also saves telemetry for your best lap of every session (once each). Both can be switched off in Settings > History sync.
  - From a command prompt: `python -m debrief sync`.
- **Live data** (Settings > Live data): while iRacing runs, Debrief records every session you drive (read only). Recorded sessions join the matching Garage 61 sessions in **All sessions** (button **From iRacing**; a session Garage 61 lacks shows as "Recorded by Debrief"), and race reviews gain a **From iRacing** section: fuel per lap and stint, driver aids and changes, incidents with their lap, your pit stops and every car's, positions by lap. With **Start with Windows** on, a recorder with an icon near the clock runs with no window (`Debrief.exe --recorder`; `--stop-recorder` asks it to quit). Recordings are in `Documents\Debrief Reviews\Recorded`.
- **Library**: one page with three tabs:
  - **Library**: your personal bests, progress over time per track and car, and the corners that keep costing you time. When you have reviews, Debrief opens on your last race.
    - Personal bests, trends, weak corners, Season and Skills count **race** sessions only, so a low-fuel practice lap never becomes a PB. Tick **Include practice and qualifying** to see those rows too.
    - **Skills**: your driver profile. Each race with at least six clean laps gets scores for braking, turn-in to apex, apex to throttle and corner exits, so you can see which phase has the most time to find lately and how it changes over the season. No Garage 61 requests.
- **Saved reviews** in the sidebar open any earlier review.
- **Add race result** (bar at the top of an open review): type in where you started and finished, incidents, iRating and safety rating from the iRacing results page. Only the finishing position is needed. You can edit or delete it later. No Garage 61 requests.
- **Goals**: set a target lap time for a track and car (for example 1:23.500). Debrief shows how close your best is and marks it achieved when you beat it. Edit or delete goals any time. No Garage 61 requests.
- **Prepare**: pick at the top: one click for the cars you've driven, or search every car by class, then a series that uses it, a track and a race week. Debrief remembers your last choice, and the star beside the series keeps it as a quick button. Below that is one view: race length, start type, rain, cautions and how often it runs; your best and typical lap there, dry and wet apart; the corners that keep costing you; extra laps from your other starred series; a **track card** (five short optional notes for what telemetry can't see, saved per track in `track-notes` in your reviews folder, so a card follows the track into every series); and racecraft and rivals once you have three scanned races there. **Build prep page** there gives your history, teammates' pace and a practice plan. **Plan fuel** works out fuel and stops for one driver; **Plan stints** (shown for races of an hour or more, and inside Plan fuel) plans a long race with up to 8 drivers, saved per track in `endurance` in your reviews folder. The season plan shows one chip per week left, marking the weeks that need the most prep; click one to go to it. Under **More**: **Download official schedule** and **Load PDF...** (once a season; Debrief reads it on your PC and never shares it), Track Intel import and prep pages for any track and car.
- **Setup Lab** (**Ctrl+7**): your setups, read from the telemetry files iRacing saves (`Documents\iRacing\telemetry`; if yours are elsewhere, set the folder in Settings > Setup Lab and Coach). Press **Read my telemetry**: the first time reads every file (a few hundred take a few minutes), then only new ones. Pick a car and track (newest first) to see:
  - **Your setups**: Setup A, B, C in the order you first drove them, with dates and files. Debrief reads the setup from the telemetry, because iRacing's .sto setup files are encrypted.
  - **What changed** between them, in plain words (a change made the same on both sides is one line).
  - **Which was faster**: only when the clean laps clearly say so (at least 5 counted laps each). Otherwise it says it can't tell yet and roughly how many more laps would. Very different track temperature or fuel is called out, never adjusted for. Driving order counts too: if the faster setup was simply driven later, part of the gain may be you getting quicker, so Debrief says so and asks you to go back to the first setup for a few laps (A, then B, then A again settles it). If your own improvement could explain most of the gap, it says the two can't be compared fairly yet.
  - **Try next**: one change at a time, with why, how to check it and how sure Debrief is (a reading, a guess, or low to medium for tyre temperatures). A fixed setup series just says so. Set **This series is** open or fixed if Debrief can't tell. Under **What the telemetry shows**: your hot tyre pressures now (and, when it differs, what they were in your fastest stints there), front against rear, fuel use and ride height.
  - **Share what changed** (under What changed): a short text of your last change and what the laps said, plain or with bold for Discord, each with **Copy**. No names or file names; Debrief never posts it.
  - **Car balance: what to try next** (2.23, at the top, no button): your clean laps are read corner by corner for understeer, oversteer, instability on the way in and poor traction on the way out. A problem is called the **setup** only when it shows on most clean laps and in several corners of the same kind; otherwise it says it looks like **driving** and points to the Corner coach. Each setup problem gets **one change at a time**, ranked, with why, what to expect and what to watch for, and how sure it is (Sure, Likely, A hint, A guess). It never pulls one part both ways and never undoes your last change unless the laps showed it slower; a fixed series only gets brake bias, traction control and ABS dials, otherwise driving advice. **How did the car feel?** (Agree or Not what I felt) is kept on this PC and moves the sureness a step. What it suggested is kept per car and track, and after your next stints Set-Up Lab says whether you did it and what its own comparison showed. A short **Car balance** card also sits at the top of each Practice session. Uses steering, speed, throttle, brake, yaw rate and sideways g (and the four wheel-speed channels for wheelspin when the file has them; without them traction is judged from how the car turns and speeds up).
  - **How the car handles**: a grid of corners (Turn 1, Turn 2...) on the way in, in the middle and on the way out, each marked **Pushes** (understeer), **Rotates** (oversteer), **Neutral** or **Not clear yet**, with a one-line summary, how often you caught the rear (countersteer) and a small cross-check note. Pushes or Rotates only shows when a shuffle test across your laps says it isn't luck and it is big enough to feel. The way in is compared with your own typical corner entry (braking always turns a car in more than steady cornering), and the note under the grid says by how much. A catch only counts when you held opposite lock while the car kept rotating, not a quick change of direction. It reads your newest setup's files (pick another setup or all of them). **Setup ideas** below it: one change at a time with your setup's own numbers, why, and **Worth a try** or **A guess**; then drive 6 clean laps and compare again. Only changes your setup can actually make are offered (a bar already at its lowest falls through to the other end of the car, and says so). A fixed setup series gets a driving note instead.
  - **Tyre pressures over time**: your runs by track temperature (5 °C bands): cold pressure, hot pressure, typical lap, and which cold pressure was quickest only when you drove the pressures back and forth (A, B, A, B) and a shuffle test agrees. One pressure per day, or one block each after the other, is listed but not compared. With 4 or more runs, **Hot pressure from cold** predicts the hot pressures for a cold front and rear pressure and a track temperature, with its typical miss, and says when the pressures you type are outside what your runs show.
  - **Notes**: readings that were left out (cold tyres, short stints) and files that couldn't be read.
  - **More > Check a setup file**: a setup from someone else (.sto or .zip) is checked for programs and scripts before you use it. Debrief never opens, runs or installs it, and keeps no copy.
  - Debrief only reads iRacing's folders; it never writes into them. Prepare shows **Setup Lab for this car** when there are files for that car and track.
- **Practice** (**Ctrl+5**): every time you drove, from the same telemetry files as Setup Lab (press **Read my telemetry** there or here), newest first: date, car, track, session, laps and best lap. Files of one car and track with no more than 3 hours between them count as one session. Open one to see:
  - **How it went**: best lap, typical lap (the middle of your clean laps), how many laps were within half a second of your best, and how it compares with your last session there.
  - **Best possible lap**: your best time through each corner added up, from clean laps once there are 5 (else from all valid laps, and it says so), with a bar for each corner where time is. A guide, not a lap you can count on.
  - **Corner coach**: from your recent laps of that car at that track (up to the last 10 files, within 30 days), one card per corner where your best laps do something clearly different: what's different, how to try it, the speeds it changes, why, and the laps it comes from, marked Clear, Likely or Few laps. A tip is only given when it passes a shuffle test (Debrief shuffles which laps count as your best 400 times; a pattern that shuffled laps show as clearly in 1 shuffle in 20 or more is "no clear pattern yet"), race laps and practice or qualifying laps are compared separately, and brake-point tips need 10 clean laps. "Your best laps were about 0.12 s quicker here" is that corner's share of the gap between your quickest laps and the rest, not a promise of lap time. Then corners with no clear pattern yet, corners already close to your best, and your braking habits (ABS, full pedal, trail braking, coasting).
  - **Laps** (counted, or why not), **Fuel and strategy**, **Conditions** and **Setups used** (with a link to Setup Lab).
  - **Compare with a teammate's lap**: pick a teammate's .ibt file for the same car and track. Debrief checks it for anything harmful, takes their best clean lap and shows Corner coach cards against it. The file is read once and never copied; tick **Keep for this car and track** to keep only the worked-out lap (tick or untick it any time; picking another file without Keep never deletes a lap you kept, and it says so). **Forget it** removes it.
  - **Coach these corners** opens the Coach: it then focuses on those corners and says each one's short tip after the corner.
  - **This session's one thing** (top of Practice): one change for your next session at that car and track, picked from the Corner coach: the corner, what to do, how, the check that counts a lap as a hit (for example "peak brake at or below 88%"), how sure it is (Clear pattern, Likely, Few laps, Experiment) and how many laps it comes from. It is never surer than the Corner coach tip behind it. Where your best laps are quicker but there's no clear reason, it says **Try this and see** (an experiment, not a known fix). **Coach this** has the live coach watch it; **Pick another** picks a different corner. After you drive, open that session: **Your one thing** counts the laps where you hit it, says whether those laps were quicker through that corner (only when a shuffle test says the gap is real, with 3 or more laps each way, each compared with laps from the same part of the session; a carry-more-speed plan is timed to 150 m past the corner) and says **Next: keep this** (under 7 laps out of 10) or **move on**. A session is scored once it has a clean lap through that corner. The list below keeps every plan and its hit rate. Plans are kept in the `practice` folder next to your settings.
  - Corners are called **Turn 3** everywhere on this page, with the race review's name (**C3**) in small text beside it.
- **Racing** (after Practice in the sidebar, no shortcut): your starts and incidents across all your races, read from your replay scans, telemetry files and saved reviews (and RaceCap's scans when linked; Debrief only reads them). Pick a track at the top when you have raced at more than one.
  - **Your starts**: a table of your races (date, start and position after lap 1, places gained or lost, where they changed, lap 1 incidents, contact Yes/Probably/Possibly/No, and where the positions came from: official results, RaceCap's results, iRacing's position from your telemetry or the replay, or a guess from the replay), with **Open review** when the race has a saved review. A bar per corner shows places gained and lost on lap 1 over your scanned races; it needs a replay scan (**Scan this race** on a review) and only counts cars the replay saw near you.
  - **Your incidents**: per track, a grid of corners against **Your own**, **Contact** and **Caught up** with counts and points, lap 1 against later and race, qualifying and practice, then every incident with when, the lap, where, the points (iRacing counts 1x off track, 2x loss of control, 4x contact), what kind, how sure, who was close (from a replay scan) and why. Points added while telemetry wasn't recording are counted but not placed.
  - It only calls something a **pattern** when at least 3 races show it and a fair test says chance can't explain it; otherwise it says what happened. **Look again** picks up new files.
- **Car vs car** (after Racing in the sidebar, no shortcut): pick a track and class, and **Races**: one season (the default, with its dates shown, because iRacing can change the cars' balance between seasons) or all races. The table shows each car's drivers, typical race lap, best lap and the gap for drivers of similar iRating, with how sure it is (**Unlikely to be chance**, **Likely** or **Not enough to tell**); a verdict ("Drivers of similar iRating were about 0.4 s a lap quicker in the X than in the Y") is only given with at least 15 drivers with an iRating in each car and a fair test that chance can't explain the gap, and car choice and driver skill can't be fully separated. Only finishers count (Running at the end, 90% of the winner's laps), each race counts once and only the same track layout is pooled. **You vs the field** places your race pace among drivers in the same car and iRating band. **Where you were faster in each car** compares your own telemetry in two cars of the class at that track, per turn and straight (laps from different sessions, days and setups; with fewer than 5 clean laps in a car, a rough look with at most 3 possible differences); a teammate's lap loaded on the Coach page or a Practice session fills in a car you haven't driven there, under their name. The numbers come from the iRacing results files you add to your race reviews (kept beside the review when you press Save result, in `field.json`: the whole field's names, iRatings and lap times stay on this PC, and sync with your library if it is in OneDrive) and replay scans with car names; nothing is fetched.
- **Coach** (**Ctrl+3**): press **Start coaching**, then drive. With iRacing running, Debrief says short cues: a brake marker on the straight before the corners your race reviews (or the Corner coach) say cost you most, a word after them, and your lap against your best ("Down two tenths"). **Coaching level** (top of the Coach page): **Quiet** says only that, at most 2 tips a lap. **Normal** (the default) says up to 4 cues a lap, 3 seconds apart: also a word after any corner where you lost a tenth or more against your reference, using what changed most ("T3, braked early", "T3: more speed, 4 down" (speeds are in mph), "T3, a tenth down"), one "T3, good, up a tenth" a lap where you gained, and the first two sectors ("Sector one, up two tenths"). With no race reviews or Corner coach cards for the car and track, it learns the focus corners from this session (the two that lost the most over your last 1 to 3 clean laps, updated every lap) and the status says "Learning from this session". **Busy**: up to 6 cues a lap, 2.5 seconds apart, 3 focus corners, two "good" cues a lap. **Only when I ask**: nothing until you ask for your pace. **How's my pace?** (**Ctrl+Shift+P**, the button on the Coach page, or a wheel button you teach it under Settings): your last clean lap against your reference and the corner that cost the most there ("Down three tenths, most in T4"), said as soon as it is safe to talk, even while muted. With no lap of yours in your telemetry files it says "Building your reference" as you roll out, and after your first clean lap "Reference set, thirty-eight nine" (**Say lap times** in Settings: Short, the default, or Full, "one thirty-eight nine"); from then on every lap is compared with it. Every cue is checked against the car once more just before it plays: a brake marker that could no longer finish a second before the brake point, a word about a corner once you are braking for the next one, or a sector time once a newer one is done is dropped (the coach log says "no longer valid"). Sector and lap times and "good" cues wait for the **hard zones** (from hard braking on your reference lap until full throttle) to pass and only start when they finish before the next one. The status line says where the reference comes from (your telemetry files or this session). It coaches from your own best clean lap for that car and track (from your telemetry files, or a better lap you drive). It stays quiet under braking, in corners, with a car alongside, on pit road, on out laps and on lap 1 of a race, and in races at all unless you switch that on. **Mute** or **Ctrl+Shift+M** (works from the car while Debrief is open; map it to a wheel button) silences it. Settings: what it says, focus corners (1 to 3), volume, speaking speed, an optional beep at the brake point, and **Test voice**. Corners from the Corner coach show as "Turn 3 (from the Corner coach)", with the tip it says after them. With a plan from Practice it also watches that one corner each clean lap, shows "Hit 3 of 5 so far" and now and then says a short word on how it went ("Good brake point"); it stays quiet when a pass is too close to the line to call, so it never contradicts the score. **Mute from a wheel button** (Settings on the Coach page): press **Learn**, then the button on your wheel within 15 seconds (one of its first 32 buttons); it mutes and unmutes the coach while it is on. **Clear** removes it. **Coach against a teammate's lap** (off by default): pick their file on the Coach page and tick **Coach against** (their name)**'s lap**; the next time you start the coach in that car at that track it measures you against their best lap, and your own new bests don't replace it. Keep Debrief open while you drive. **Voice and sound** (Coach page): pick one of six voices: George (British male, the default), Michael (American male), Fenrir (American male), Heart (American female), Kumo (Japanese accent, male) or Omega (Indian accent, male), and press **Preview**; pick the device it plays through (**Play through**; an unplugged device falls back to Windows default); **Measure audio delay** so each cue starts that much earlier; **Add your own voice pack** from a folder or .zip (checked first: only sound clips and a manifest). Windows speech says only the rare phrase a voice can't. **Repeat that** (**Ctrl+Shift+R**, or a wheel button) and **Mute for 1 minute** (**Ctrl+Shift+N**). A focus corner you got wrong and then drove well twice in a row is left alone until it slips again. **Rehearse with a session** plays one of your Practice sessions through the coach at real speed (not while coaching live). **Coach log**: every cue made, said or dropped and why, one file per session (last 30 kept, in `coach-logs` next to your settings, no personal data); **Copy for Claude** copies a short summary. Debrief never changes iRacing's volume, and nothing goes online. **Driving view** (button on the Coach page) shows COACHING, MUTED, REHEARSING or OFF in big type with your one thing, the last 3 cues and your gap to your reference lap, to read from the car; it follows Settings > Units.
- **Corner notes and Learning the track** (2.20): on the Coach page, hold **Ctrl+Shift+V** (or a wheel button you teach it under Settings) while you drive and say a short note; let go to save it (rising tone: listening, falling tone: saved, low tone: thrown away). The coach plays it before that corner on your next laps (every lap for 5 laps, then every third lap, then only when that corner lost time), never under braking and never in a corner. **Practice > Corner notes** plays, re-records, moves, switches off and deletes them (30 days in the trash) and has **Copy summary**; the Coach page has a **Mic test**, a red **Recording** light and a **Notes only** level. With no reference lap yet your first clean lap becomes it ("Learning the track: N clean laps yet"), each quicker clean lap replaces it (never an off-track or incident lap), **Ctrl+Shift+L** uses the lap you just finished, and the coach says how each corner compares with your best so far ("T5, 3 down on your best", "T3, braked 15 metres later", "T4, off track and 5 faster in").
- **Units** (**Settings > Units**): mph (the default) or km/h. Every page, the coach's spoken speeds and the saved review pages use it.
- **Reviews** open with **What to work on**: a two-line verdict, up to three corners to **fix first** (what to change there and roughly what it's worth), then **Fix these** (your habits as cards, biggest cost first, each with what happened, what it costs and a **Try:** for next race; the numbers are in **Show the numbers**), a **Corner by corner** strip (braking, mid-corner and exit for each turn), **Keep doing** and **Worth a look**, then the **race story** (lap times). Every chart and table is still there under **All the numbers**.
- **Updates**: Debrief checks GitHub for a newer version once a day (on by default; switch it off in Settings > Updates). **Update now** downloads the installer, checks its SHA-256 against the one published with the release (Settings > Updates says "checksum"; once a signing key is published it also checks the signature and says so), installs it and reopens Debrief. Before an update re-draws your saved reviews, Debrief copies every review.json to `.backup` in your reviews folder (the two newest sets are kept).
- **Privacy**: Debrief talks only to Garage 61 (your sync and reviews) and GitHub (the daily update check). Fonts ship inside the app.
- **Multi-class races**: a review lists every teammate and followed driver who was in the same session, in any car or class (1 extra request; Garage 61 doesn't share the rest of the field). In a multi-class race, clean laps that lost their time in one short stretch (usually traffic) are left out of your pace and consistency numbers and listed in the review.
- **Save as picture** (2.25): the Team page boards, corner kings, head-to-heads, members, this week and season sections, and every member page, have a **Save as picture** button that makes a 1200 x 675 .png card (leaderboard, head-to-head, corner king, driver snapshot or new team best) for Discord or socials. It is saved in the `shares` folder inside your reviews folder, Debrief says where, and **Open folder** shows it.
- **Team member pages** (2.22): on the Team page click a member's name, in the **Members** strip, a leaderboard or a head-to-head, to see their best laps against yours, the corners where they are quicker and their latest laps. It uses only what the Team page already saved, so it asks Garage 61 for nothing; an empty part says why.
- **Team**: the hub for the Garage 61 teams you are in. Its laps come from those teams, in any car; nothing about a team is typed in or built in, and the members are whatever Garage 61 lists (until you are in a team the page shows how to join and your own sections still work). Click **Refresh team data** to also sync your teammates' practice, qualifying and race laps to this PC (2.24; if Garage 61 says slow down it stops and the next click continues), and to get:
  - **Leaderboards**: everyone's best clean lap at your six most recent tracks and cars, with you highlighted.
  - **Corner kings**: who is quickest through each corner, from each driver's best lap with telemetry (up to five drivers per track and car; the page says who was left out). Every teammate shows their iRating.
  - **Head-to-heads**: you against each teammate. The gap is your best minus theirs, so a minus number means you were quicker.
  The page says when it was last refreshed, when it was already up to date, and exactly how many Garage 61 requests the refresh used. After you change the team in Settings it says the page is out of date until you refresh.

Only one job runs at a time (a review, a prep page or a team refresh). If you close the window while one is running, Debrief finishes it before it shuts down.

Reviews are saved in `Documents\Debrief Reviews`. You can open them in a browser too: double-click `index.html` there. The team hub is in the `team` folder and prep pages are in the `prep` folder; they open the same way.

From a command prompt you can also run `python -m debrief team` or `python -m debrief prep --track ID --car ID`.

Setup checklist: until everything is set up, the start page (and the welcome screen, before Garage 61 is connected) shows what is done and the next step, telemetry first: find the telemetry folder, turn on telemetry in iRacing (Alt+L), read it, drive 10 clean laps in one session (what Practice needs to pick one thing to work on), test the coach's voice, then connect Garage 61. Each step has a button where there is one to press (Settings, Read my telemetry, Practice, Test voice). **Hide** it whenever you like; **Help > Setup checklist** shows it again. Until Garage 61 is connected only Progress, Season, Skills, Goals, All sessions and Team wait for it; a token Garage 61 turned down unlocks nothing.

Keyboard: **Ctrl+Enter** reviews your last race, **Ctrl+1** to **Ctrl+9** and **Ctrl+0** open the pages in sidebar order (Today, Prepare, Coach, All sessions, Practice, Racing, Setup Lab, Car vs car, Progress, Team), **F1** or **?** shows the list of keys, **Ctrl+,** opens Settings, **Ctrl+Shift+M** mutes the coach, **Ctrl+Shift+R** repeats its last cue, **Ctrl+Shift+N** mutes it for 1 minute, **Ctrl+Shift+P** asks it for your pace.

## Comparison laps

The Garage 61 API only shares laps from **you, your teammates, and drivers you follow**. There's no global search. To get comparisons, follow a few quick drivers in the cars you race on Garage 61, or have buddies join your team. Debrief picks up to three: a main comparison about 0.8s quicker than you, the fastest one, and one close to your pace.

## Staying within Garage 61's rules

- Personal, non-commercial use only.
- Debrief never polls Garage 61 in a loop. By default it does two things on its own: when it opens it catches up your history (only new laps, usually a few requests), and once a day it asks GitHub (not Garage 61) whether there's a newer version. Turn them off in Settings > History sync (**Sync each time Debrief opens**) and Settings > Updates (**Check for updates when Debrief opens**). Everything else happens only when you click something.
- At least one second between requests, and it waits if Garage 61 says to slow down.
- Everything is cached in the `.cache` folder inside Debrief (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Debrief\.cache` for the installed app), so a lap is never downloaded twice. A new race is about 30 requests; re-opening it costs none.
- Team refresh: at most about 60 requests the first time (six tracks and cars); clicking it again within ten minutes uses none, and later refreshes only download what's new.
- Prep page: a handful of requests (teammates' best laps at that track and car).
- Review: one more request to list teammates and followed drivers who were in the same session.
- Prepare page and track cards: none (the schedule comes from iRacing's PDF; downloading it is one request to iRacing, only when you click).
- Requests are spaced about a second apart; if Garage 61 asks Debrief to slow down, later requests in that run are spaced further apart.
- Goals, race results, Season, Practice, Setup Lab and the Coach use no requests at all.

## Teams on Garage 61

Debrief's team features use whatever Garage 61 teams you are in; no team names are built into Debrief. The Team hub, the weekly leaderboards and the teammates' laps on Prep pages come from those teams, so you need to be in at least one:

1. Make your Garage 61 account and connect iRacing (step 4 of the install).
2. Ask someone in a team to invite you to it on Garage 61, and accept the invite.
3. In Debrief, open **Team** and press **Refresh**.

Until you're in a team, the Team page, Prep pages and weekly leaderboards show a "Join a team on Garage 61" panel instead of empty boards. Your own reviews, Library, Season, Skills, Goals and Prepare work without it. **Settings > Team and extras > Team** shows the teams Debrief sees you in.

The optional iRacing API credentials (`iracing_client_id` / `iracing_client_secret` in settings.toml) are still read if present, but no longer shown in Settings: nothing in Debrief needs them today, and typing a race result works without them.

## Keeping your token safe

- The token lives only in `settings.toml` in this folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Debrief` for the installed app). Debrief never shows it again after you save it.
- Don't share settings.toml. If you give a buddy a copy of the Debrief folder, delete settings.toml from the copy first.
- If it ever leaks, create a new token at garage61.net/developer and paste the new one in Settings.

## Troubleshooting

- Problems are written to `debrief.log` in this folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Debrief` for the installed app).
- **"Garage 61 rejected the token (401)"**: paste the token again in Settings > Garage 61 token and click **Save and check**.
- **"Telemetry download: NOT available"**: some telemetry may need a Garage 61 Pro plan. Your own laps normally work.

## Building the installer

GitHub Actions builds it on every push (`.github/workflows/build.yml`): it runs the tests, builds `Debrief.exe` with PyInstaller (`packaging/debrief.spec`), checks it with `Debrief.exe --selftest`, builds `Debrief-Setup-<version>.exe` with Inno Setup (`packaging/debrief.iss`), test-installs it silently and uploads it as a build artifact. Pushing a tag like `v2.0.0` also publishes a GitHub Release with the installer attached. The tag must match `__version__` in `debrief/__init__.py`.

To build on your own Windows PC:

```
pip install -r requirements.txt pyinstaller
pyinstaller packaging/debrief.spec --noconfirm --clean
dist\Debrief\Debrief.exe --selftest
iscc /DAppVersion=2.0.0 packaging\debrief.iss
```

The installer appears in `dist-installer`. The source setup above (`dev\Setup.bat`, settings.toml next to the code) keeps working; only the installed app uses `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Debrief`.
